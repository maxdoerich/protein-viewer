00:00-00:07  [1-210]
  Gas vesicles are hollow, gas-filled protein shells that let bacteria and archaea float.

00:07-00:13  [210-390]
  The wall is only one protein layer thick. It is built from thousands of copies of one small protein, GvpA, arranged in ribs.

00:14-00:20  [425-600]
  GvpA folds into an alpha-beta-beta-alpha motif: two helices (red) and a beta-hairpin (yellow).

00:20-00:25  [595-760]
  The side facing the gas is strongly hydrophobic (gold) and the outer surface is hydrophilic (blue). Gas passes through small pores, but water is kept out.

00:26-00:31  [790-935]
  GvpA copies pack side by side into ribs, which gives the wall its corrugated shape.

00:32-00:36  [955-1075]
  Cryo-EM density of the wall (EMD-14238) with the fitted atomic model (PDB 7R1C).

00:37-00:42  [1100-1270]
  Repeating this arrangement builds the whole shell: a cylinder closed by two conical tips.

00:42-00:46  [1270-1370]
  Each half of the shell is one continuous, low-pitch helix of GvpA. The two halves have opposite polarity and meet at a central seam.

00:47-00:54  [1420-1630]
  GvpC binds to the outside along the ribs and forms a helical cage that reinforces the shell.
